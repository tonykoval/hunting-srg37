Appendix D The Brief — a page to hand to a mathematician or a model

Everything a fresh mind needs to attack the classification of $\mathrm{SRG}(37,18,8,9)$ without repeating two years of this project: the exact question, the walls with their measured widths, what has been refuted, what a winning result must look like, and a copy-and-paste prompt. Written 2026-09-24; the numbers are those of Chapter 10.5.

D.1   The question, exactly

Classify the strongly regular graphs with parameters $(37,18,8,9)$, equivalently the regular two-graphs on $38$ points, equivalently the symmetric conference matrices of order $38$ (Seidel matrix $S$: symmetric, zero diagonal, $\pm1$ off the diagonal, $S^2=37I$), equivalently the equiangular tight frames of $38$ lines in $\mathbb R^{19}$. Known: $6{,}802$ graphs from $194$ two-graphs (Maksimović–Rukavina 2022, building on McKay–Spence 2001). Open since 2001: is the list complete?

D.2   What is proved

  • Rigidity. Every regular two-graph on $38$ points outside the $194$ known is rigid, hence every unknown $\mathrm{SRG}(37,18,8,9)$ has trivial automorphism group (Ch 10.5.1). So no symmetry-based method — orbit matrices, prescribed automorphisms, switching from symmetric seeds, character or frame engines, trace identities — can produce a new one.
  • Gauge. Every class has a carrier with vertex $0$ isolated whose row-lex-min labelling lies in the all-zero cube of the SAT tree ("tower 0"); with the fleet ledgers merged that tower still holds $106{,}955$ leaves that resisted the depth-$40$ budget, at more than $4$ h each (Ch 10.5.2).
  • Forced completion. Fix a vertex $v$ and its neighbourhood $H=N(v)$, an $8$-regular graph on $18$ vertices. If $H$ is the neighbourhood of a true graph, the rest is forced through the star complement of $N[v]$ (two completions, $0.15$ s). So classification $=$ enumeration of admissible $H$ (Ch 11).

D.5   What would win

A hereditary necessary condition: a property of the induced subgraph on any $m\le18$ vertices of a true neighbourhood (or of any principal $m\times m$ block of a symmetric conference matrix of order $38$) that fails for a large fraction of partial graphs satisfying interlacing. To change the arithmetic it must cut the generation tree by three or more orders of magnitude in total; at $10^3$ the neighbourhood route becomes a fleet job of weeks, at $10^2$ it is still years. The natural source is the structure the norm-square condition touches: $37I-A_X^2$ must be the Gram matrix of $19$ vectors in $\{\pm1\}^{38-m}$, and Gram matrices of $\pm1$ vectors carry more than a determinant condition (Cauchy–Binet sums of squared minors, congruences of minors mod powers of $2$, representability). A characterization of $\pm1$-representable Gram matrices that survives restriction to sub-blocks would be exactly the lever. Second candidates: a lattice-theoretic obstruction over $\mathbb Z[\tfrac{1+\sqrt{37}}2]$ on the rank-$19$ frame lattice of $S$ that is local in the rows; or a forbidden induced-subgraph theorem for neighbourhoods of conference graphs.

Anything that is not hereditary is a leaf filter and cannot help: the width is in the generation, not in the completion.

D.6   The prompt

Copy from here to the end of the block. It is written for a strong reasoning model; a human reader can skip it.

You are asked for NEW necessary conditions in algebraic graph theory, to be tested by computer within the hour.

Setting. Gamma is a strongly regular graph with parameters (37,18,8,9): 37 vertices, degree 18, adjacent
vertices have 8 common neighbours, non-adjacent 9. Its eigenvalues are 18 and (-1 +- sqrt 37)/2, each with
multiplicity 18. Adding an isolated vertex and taking the Seidel matrix S = J - I - 2A on 38 points gives a
symmetric conference matrix: S symmetric, zero diagonal, +-1 elsewhere, S^2 = 37 I. Every such Gamma is a
descendant of such an S and conversely. Known: 6,802 such graphs (194 two-graphs); every further one has
trivial automorphism group (proved). The classification is open.

Fix a vertex v of Gamma and let H = N(v): an 8-regular graph on 18 vertices. Once H is known, Gamma is
forced (star complement). The classification therefore reduces to enumerating the admissible H, where
"admissible" currently means: for X = {v} u H (19 points), both matrices
    G_theta = I + (theta/18) A_X - ((1+theta)/18)(J - I - A_X),   theta = (-1 +- sqrt 37)/2,
are positive semidefinite. The number of admissible H up to isomorphism is about 10^12 (measured), of which
about 3*10^5 extend to a graph. Generating 10^12 graphs is the wall.

What is already known and must not be re-proposed: (1) Cauchy interlacing / PSD of the cone blocks (used);
(2) intersection caps (vacuous); (3) the switching-spectrum condition: the eigenvalues of the non-neighbourhood
are theta or -1-theta for each eigenvalue theta of H (automatic given (1)); (4) 2-adic congruences of
characteristic polynomials of Seidel matrices (Greaves-Yatsyna-Syatriadi-Phan) and the 2-adic valuation
v_2 det(37I - A^2) >= 2m-2 on principal blocks of order m (automatic); (5) the F_37-rank bound rank <= 19 (only
for blocks of order >= 20); (6) the NORM-SQUARE condition: for any 19-subset X of the 38 points,
det(37 I - A_X^2) = det(B)^2 with B a 19x19 (+-1)-matrix, so for H: det(37 I - S_H^2)/36 is a perfect square
divisible by 2^34, or zero. It removes 98.4 % of admissible H but is NOT hereditary.

Wanted: a necessary condition on the induced subgraph of H on m < 18 vertices (equivalently on an m x m
principal block of S with m <= 19), strictly stronger than PSD/interlacing, that a large fraction of
interlacing-feasible partial graphs violate. Candidates should come with a proof or a precise derivation
from S^2 = 37 I, the +-1 structure (37 I - A_X^2 = B B^T with B a +-1 matrix of size m x (38-m)), the rank-18
idempotents, or lattice/ code structure over Z[(1+sqrt37)/2] or F_37. State each condition as an explicit
computable test on a 0/1 adjacency matrix of size m, say for which m it applies, and say why it is not
implied by (1)-(6). Conditions that only apply to the complete H (m = 18) are leaf filters and are of
secondary interest; say so if that is what you have.

We will test every proposal on 6,802 true neighbourhoods (must pass 100 %) and on 10,000 generated
admissible ones (pass rate = the value of the idea) before believing any of it.

D.7   How a proposal is tested here

The harness is scripts/srg37_switch_spectrum_test.py and scripts/srg37_square_test.py in the orbit-gen repository: each condition is coded as a function of the $18\times18$ adjacency matrix (or of an $m\times m$ induced block), run on the vertex-$0$ neighbourhoods of the $6{,}802$ catalogue graphs and on the generated admissible graphs of out/f3bat2/geng37_shard0_4096.g6, and reported as two pass rates. A condition that fails a single true neighbourhood is wrong; one that passes every generated one is implied by what we have. The exact leaf test used for "admissible" is the C function in tools/srg37_width/wsample.c, which is also the sampler that measured $W$.

Reading. The model is a real collaborator — it corrected an error in a brief on a sibling problem, gave witnesses that check to the digit, and was more careful about unsupported rejection rates than the first version of this page. What it has not produced, and what D.5 still asks for, is a hereditary condition that bites before about ten vertices. The Gram-type family provably cannot.
